PLAINTIFF'S MOTION TO COMPEL IMMEDIATE SERVICE OF THE
FINAL ORDER AND JUDGMENT AND FOR CLARIFICATION OF THE
DOCKET RECORD PURSUANT TO FED. R. CIV. P. 77(d) AND 79(a)

TO THE HONORABLE JUDGE OF SAID COURT:

Plaintiff Matthew Oliver Reardon, proceeding pro se, respectfully moves this Court
to compel the Clerk to immediately serve notice of entry and a copy of any order
or judgment resolving Plaintiff's Objections (Dkt. 90, filed July 28, 2026) to the
Magistrate Judge's July 14, 2026 Memorandum Opinion, Order, and Recommendation
(Dkt. 89), and states as follows:

1. On or about August 10, 2026, Plaintiff learned — through means outside the
   formal docket notification process — that this case has been dismissed in its
   entirety. Plaintiff has not been served with, and has not received by mail,
   email, ECF notification, or any other means, a copy of the district judge's
   written order or the final judgment entered in this matter.

2. Plaintiff timely filed nine specific objections (Dkt. 90) to the Magistrate
   Judge's recommendation on July 28, 2026, fourteen days after entry of the
   Memorandum Opinion, Order, and Recommendation, as required. Plaintiff is
   entitled to know, and the public docket is entitled to reflect, the district
   judge's disposition of each of those nine objections — not merely the bottom-
   line result.

3. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 77(d)(1) provides: "Immediately after entering
   an order or judgment, the clerk must serve notice of the entry, as provided
   in Rule 5(b), on each party who is not in default for failing to appear, and
   must record the service on the docket." Plaintiff has received no such notice.

4. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 79(a) requires the Clerk to keep a docket for
   each case and to enter every order and judgment "chronologically" and
   "concisely." Plaintiff requests that the Clerk confirm on the record (a) the
   exact date the order/judgment was signed, (b) the exact date it was entered
   on the docket, and (c) the date, method, and address used for any attempted
   service on Plaintiff.

5. Because Plaintiff proceeds pro se and receives no ECF notifications by
   default, and because Plaintiff's mailing address of record may not be his
   current, reliable address given his documented housing instability, Plaintiff
   requests that the Clerk additionally send the order/judgment by email to the
   address Plaintiff has used throughout this litigation, in addition to first-
   class mail, so as to avoid any dispute later about the date of actual notice.

6. Plaintiff raises this promptly and specifically because the time to note an
   appeal under Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(1)(A) runs from entry of judgment, not from
   receipt of notice — and Rule 77(d) itself states that "[l]ack of notice of
   the entry does not affect the time for appeal or relate back to it," except
   for the narrow reopening procedure in Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(6). Plaintiff is
   entitled to the information necessary to protect that right, and the Clerk's
   failure to serve notice as required by Rule 77(d)(1) should not be permitted
   to erode it by delay.

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court order the Clerk to
immediately serve Plaintiff with a copy of the order and judgment disposing of
Dkt. 90, to confirm the exact dates of signing and entry, and for such other
relief as the Court deems just.
